Your health data, kept at home.

Homeostat pulls your WHOOP, Garmin and FreeStyle Libre data into one SQLite file on your own machine, then gives you charts with evidence, a run map that works offline, goals that keep their history, and an AI you can question about your own body — with every number cited.

Nothing goes to a cloud we run. There is none.

01

One file.

Everything lives in a single SQLite database on your disk. Copy it, back it up, open it with any SQLite tool.

02

One writer.

A small daemon is the only process that writes to it. It serves the web app on localhost and syncs your connectors.

03

No cloud.

We do not run a server. Nothing to sign up for, nothing to breach. Whether data ever leaves your machine is your call, per engine.

How it works

Sources in. Evidence out.

in Connectors

  • WHOOP recovery, sleep, strain, workouts
  • Garmin Connect workouts with GPS run tracks
  • FreeStyle Libre continuous glucose

A workout recorded by both WHOOP and Garmin is merged at read time. The raw rows stay untouched.

db.sqlite

out Interfaces

  • Web app served by the daemon on localhost
  • Ask chat with your data, every number cited
  • MCP server your own agents, scoped read access
  • FHIR R4 export take the data anywhere

Explore

Charts of every metric. Each number links to the raw measurement it was computed from, so a chart is never the end of the trail.

Training

A run map on an offline vector basemap, downloaded once for the areas you run in. No tile server is called afterwards.

Goals

Targets with computed progress and an undo history. A citation to a goal pins the target it was measured against.

Day model

Each day summarised from its own measurements: sleep, recovery, glucose, workouts. The unit of comparison across weeks.

Ask

An AI that has to show its work.

Ask questions about your own data in plain language. Every number in an answer carries a citation to the measurement it came from. A citation that does not resolve is flagged not in evidence, in the answer, where you can see it.

Descriptive only. Homeostat will tell you what your data says; it will not give medical or dosing advice.

Under the panel

Boring on purpose.

Storage
One SQLite file. The daemon is its only writer.
Secrets
Connector credentials encrypted at rest with AES-256-GCM.
Logs
Health values and prompts are never written to logs.
Network
The daemon listens on 127.0.0.1. Remote AI engines are opt-in, per engine, enforced server-side.
Agents
An MCP server exposes your data to your own agents with scoped access.
Portability
FHIR R4 export. Leave whenever you like, with everything.

On the name

home + stat + homeostasis

Home, because it lives on your machine. Stat, because it keeps your numbers. Homeostasis, because the point of looking at the numbers is a body that holds its own balance.

And the Homeostat itself: in 1948 W. Ross Ashby built a machine of four units, each a magnet-and-needle circuit wired to the other three. Disturb one and the whole thing searched its own settings until every needle came to rest again. It was among the first machines to regulate itself. We liked that the name was already taken by something that did only one job, well.

Four units, each correcting the others. Ashby, 1948.

Status
